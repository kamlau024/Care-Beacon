# Exercises after Surgery

Information for people who have lymph nodes removed (axillary dissection) as part of their treatment for breast cancer.

Page Content

**This information should not be used to diagnose yourself or in place of a doctor's care.**

**Revised February 2022**

### Should I exercise after my lymph node surgery?

Yes. For most people, aerobic exercise, like walking, and strength training (lifting weights) is safe and recommended. Exercising can help with treatment side effects and joint stiffness.

Before exercising, you should check with your health care team. Some people may not be able to do certain types of exercise.

Watch [**Exercises for Individuals with Breast Cancer Overview**](http://media.phsa.ca/home/iframe?url=BCCA/bccahealth%5cVideo1-Overview_20210130 "opens video called exercises for individuals with breast cancer overview in new tab")

### What type of exercise should I do?

First, follow the advice from your health care team.

For most people, the exercises in the handouts and videos below are safe.

### Where can I find more information?

  * Visit our [**Exercise Support page**](http://www.bccancer.bc.ca/health-info/coping-with-cancer/exercise-support "opens bc cancer exercise support page in new tab") for more information about exercising during and after cancer treatment.

  * Talk to your health care team.

  * Call [**HealthLink BC**](https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/ "opens healthlink bc page in new tab")**** at 8-1-1 for free. You can speak with an exercise professional trained in oncology (cancer).
  * [**Find a Clinical Exercise Physiotherapist**](http://bcphysio.org/find-a-physio "opens bcphysio.org webpage in new tab")

    * Search your area for a physiotherapist with an Area of Practice in oncology.
