# Chemotherapy for vaginal cancer

_Last medical review: September 2023_

Chemotherapy uses drugs to destroy cancer cells. These drugs target rapidly dividing cells throughout the whole body. This means that chemotherapy destroys cancer cells but it can also damage healthy cells.

With most types of chemotherapy, the drugs travel through the blood to reach and destroy cancer cells all over the body, including cells that may have broken away from the primary tumour. This is called systemic therapy.

Chemotherapy is sometimes used to treat vaginal cancer. If you have chemotherapy, your healthcare team will use what they know about the cancer and about your health to plan the drugs, doses and schedules.

Chemotherapy may be the only treatment you have or it may be used along with other cancer treatments. You may have chemotherapy to:

  * shrink a tumour before surgery (called neoadjuvant chemotherapy)
  * treat vaginal cancer that has come back
  * treat vaginal cancer when surgery or radiation therapy can't be used
  * relieve pain or control the symptoms of advanced vaginal cancer (called palliative chemotherapy)

Chemotherapy and radiation therapy is often given during the same time period to treat vaginal cancer. This is called chemoradiation. Some chemotherapy drugs can help make radiation work better by making cancer cells more sensitive to radiation. Combining chemotherapy and radiation therapy can make radiation therapy more effective than using it alone.

## Chemotherapy drugs used for vaginal cancer

The most common chemotherapy drugs for vaginal cancer include:

  * cisplatin
  * carboplatin
  * 5-fluorouracil (also called 5-FU or fluorouracil)
  * docetaxel
  * irinotecan
  * paclitaxel

## Side effects of chemotherapy

Side effects of chemotherapy will depend mainly on the drug, the dose, how it's given and your overall health. Tell your healthcare team if you have side effects that you think are from chemotherapy. The sooner you tell them of any problems, the sooner they can suggest ways to help you deal with them.

Common side effects of chemotherapy drugs for vaginal cancer include:

  * [low blood cell counts](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/low-blood-cell-counts#ci_low_blood_cell_counts_89_122_00)
  * [nausea and vomiting](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/nausea-and-vomiting#ci_nausea_and_vomiting_89_167_00)
  * a [sore mouth and throat](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/sore-mouth-and-throat#ci_sore_mouth_and_throat_89_5308_00)
  * [loss of appetite](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/loss-of-appetite#ci_loss_of_appetite_89_163_00)
  * [diarrhea](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/diarrhea#ci_diarrhea_89_5256_00)
  * [fatigue](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/fatigue#ci_fatigue_89_121_00)
  * [hair loss](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/hair-loss#ci_hair_loss_89_126_00)
  * [skin problems](https://cancer.ca/en/treatments/side-effects/skin-problems#ci_skin_problems_89_11013_00)

**radiation therapy:**

Treatment that uses high-energy rays or particles, such as x-rays or gamma rays, which produce charged ions or molecules to damage or destroy cancer cells.

Radiation therapy may be given from outside the body as external beam radiation . It may also be given as brachytherapy or systemic radiation therapy .

Also called radiotherapy, x-ray therapy and irradiation.
