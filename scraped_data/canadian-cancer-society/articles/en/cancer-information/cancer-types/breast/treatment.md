# Treatments for breast cancer

Your healthcare team will create a treatment plan just for you. The plan is based on your health and specific information about the cancer. What you want is also important when planning treatment. When deciding which treatments to offer for breast cancer, your healthcare team will consider:

  * the stage
  * if you have reached menopause
  * the hormone receptor status of the cancer
  * the HER2 status of the cancer
  * the risk that the cancer will come back, or recur (for early-stage breast cancer)
  * your overall health

Surgery is the main treatment for breast cancer. Other treatments are also used in combination to treat breast cancer, including radiation therapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy and chemotherapy.

## Treatments for ductal carcinoma in situ

Ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) is usually treated with surgery, radiation therapy and hormone therapy.

[ Learn more on Treatments for ductal carcinoma in situ](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/ductal-carcinoma-in-situ-dcis)

## Treatments for early-stage breast cancer

Early-stage breast cancer is often treated with surgery, radiation therapy, hormone therapy and targeted therapy. Chemotherapy may also be used.

[ Learn more on Treatments for early-stage breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/early-stage)

## Treatments for locally advanced breast cancer

Locally advanced breast cancer is often treated with chemotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy, surgery and radiation therapy.

[ Learn more on Treatments for locally advanced breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/locally-advanced)

## Treatments for metastatic breast cancer

Metastatic breast cancer is usually treated with hormone therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, radiation therapy and surgery.

[ Learn more on Treatments for metastatic breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/metastatic)

## Treatments for recurrent breast cancer

Recurrent breast cancer is treated with surgery, radiation therapy, hormone therapy, chemotherapy and targeted therapy.

[ Learn more on Treatments for recurrent breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/recurrent)

## Treatments for triple-negative breast cancer

Surgery and chemotherapy are the main treatments for triple-negative breast cancer. You may also be offered radiation therapy and immunotherapy.

[ Learn more on Treatments for triple-negative breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/triple-negative-breast-cancer)

## Surgery for breast cancer

Surgery is a medical procedure to examine, remove or repair tissue. Breast cancer is usually treated with surgery.

[ Learn more on Surgery for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/surgery)

## Risk of recurrence and drug treatment after surgery

Characteristics of breast cancer can help your healthcare team understand the risk of breast cancer coming back after surgery.

[ Learn more on Risk of recurrence and drug treatment after surgery](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/risk-of-recurrence)

## Radiation therapy for breast cancer

Radiation therapy uses high-energy rays to destroy cancer cells. Breast cancer is often treated with radiation therapy.

[ Learn more on Radiation therapy for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/radiation-therapy)

## Hormone therapy for breast cancer

Hormone therapy affects hormones that cancer cells need to grow. Breast cancer is often treated with hormone therapy.

[ Learn more on Hormone therapy for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/hormone-therapy)

## Chemotherapy for breast cancer

Chemotherapy uses anticancer drugs to destroy cancer cells. Breast cancer is often treated with chemotherapy.

[ Learn more on Chemotherapy for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/chemotherapy)

## Targeted therapy for breast cancer

Targeted therapy uses drugs to target specific molecules on cancer cells. Breast cancer is sometimes treated with targeted therapy.

[ Learn more on Targeted therapy for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/targeted-therapy)

## Immunotherapy for breast cancer

Breast cancer is sometimes treated with immunotherapy. It helps strengthen the immune system to fight cancer.

[ Learn more on Immunotherapy for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/immunotherapy)

## Follow-up after treatment for breast cancer

Follow-up is an important part of care for breast cancer. It often involves regular tests and visits with the healthcare team.

[ Learn more on Follow-up after treatment for breast cancer](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/breast/treatment/follow-up)

**menopause:**

The time in a woman’s life when her ovaries stop producing estrogen and she has not had a menstrual period for 12 months. Most women start menopause between 45 and 55 years of age.

Menopausal means referring to or having to do with menopause, as in menopausal symptoms.

Sometimes referred to as change of life.

See also premenopause , perimenopause and post-menopause .
