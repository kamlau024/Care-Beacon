# If ovarian cancer spreads

_Last medical review: January 2025_

Cancer cells can spread from the ovaries, fallopian tubes or peritoneum to other parts of the body. This spread is called metastasis.

Understanding how a type of cancer usually grows and spreads helps your healthcare team plan your treatment and future care. If ovarian, fallopian tube or primary peritoneal cancer spreads, it can spread to the following:

  * ovary, if it started in the fallopian tube
  * fallopian tube, if it started in the ovary
  * peritoneum
  * uterus
  * cervix
  * vagina
  * lymph nodes in the pelvis and retroperitoneum
  * bladder
  * intestine
  * [liver](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/liver-metastases#ci_liver_metastases_28_3928_00)
  * [lungs](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/lung-metastases#ci_lung_metastases_28_3926_00)
  * spleen
  * [bone](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/bone-metastases#ci_bone_metastases_28_3676_00)
  * [brain](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/brain-metastases#ci_brain_metastases_28_3925_00)

**retroperitoneum:**

The area in the back of the abdomen that is outside or behind the peritoneum (the membrane that lines the walls of the abdomen and pelvis, and covers and supports most of the organs in the abdomen).

Some of the organs contained in the retroperitoneum include the pancreas, adrenal glands and kidneys.

Also called retroperitoneal space.
