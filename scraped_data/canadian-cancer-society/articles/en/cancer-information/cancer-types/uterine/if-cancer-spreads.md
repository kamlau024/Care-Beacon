# If uterine cancer spreads

_Last medical review: March 2025_

Cancer cells can spread from the uterus to other parts of the body. This spread is called metastasis.

Understanding how a type of cancer usually grows and spreads helps your healthcare team plan your treatment and future care. If uterine cancer spreads, it can spread to the:

  * tissues around the uterus
  * vagina
  * fallopian tubes and ovaries
  * lymph nodes in the pelvis (called pelvic lymph nodes)
  * bladder
  * rectum
  * peritoneum
  * omentum
  * lymph nodes around the aorta (called para-aortic lymph nodes)
  * lymph nodes above the collarbone
  * [liver](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/liver-metastases#ci_liver_metastases_28_3928_00)
  * [lungs](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/lung-metastases#ci_lung_metastases_28_3926_00)
  * [bones](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/bone-metastases#ci_bone_metastases_28_3676_00)
  * [brain](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/metastatic/brain-metastases#ci_brain_metastases_28_3925_00)

**peritoneum:**

The membrane that lines the walls of the abdomen and pelvis (parietal peritoneum), and covers and supports most of the abdominal organs (visceral peritoneum).



**omentum:**

A fold in the peritoneum (the membrane that lines the walls of the abdomen and pelvis) that covers and supports organs and blood vessels in the abdomen.

The greater omentum hangs down from the bottom of the stomach, covers the small intestine and attaches to the transverse colon (the middle section of the large intestine). The lesser omentum is attached to the top of the stomach and bottom of the liver.
