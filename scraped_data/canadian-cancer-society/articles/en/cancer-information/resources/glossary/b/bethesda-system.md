# Bethesda system

## Description

A system used to describe cell changes in the lining of the cervix (the lower, narrow part of the uterus, or womb) for reporting Pap test results.

The Bethesda system is based on the appearance of squamous cells and glandular cells under a microscope, including the size, shape and behaviour of cells. It also describes if cancer cells are found in the cervix.

* * *

**Pap test:**

A procedure in which cells are scraped from the cervix (the lower, narrow part of the uterus, or womb) and examined under a microscope.

The Pap test is used to detect precancerous changes to the cervix and cervical cancer.

Also called Papanicolaou test or Papanikolaou test.



**squamous cells:**

A type of epithelial cell that is thin and flat and looks like a fish scale.

Squamous cells are found in the epithelium that makes up the surface of the skin. They are in the epithelium lining of organs such as the mouth, pharynx, esophagus, anus, cervix and vagina. Squamous cells also make up the lining of blood vessels and hollow areas of the body (called cavities).



**glandular cells:**

A specialized cell that releases substances (such as mucus, hormones and lubricating fluids) in the body.
