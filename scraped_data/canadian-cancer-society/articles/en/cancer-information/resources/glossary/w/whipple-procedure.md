# Whipple procedure

## Description

A surgical procedure to remove all or part of the

pancreas

Close

pancreas

The long, tapered organ behind the stomach that makes digestive juices and passes them into the duodenum (the first part of the small intestine) through the pancreatic duct. It also makes hormones (such as [insulin](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/resources/glossary/i/insulin)) that help to regulate how the body stores and uses food.

_Pancreatic_ means referring to or having to do with the pancreas, as in pancreatic cancer.

along with part of the

stomach

Close

stomach

The hollow, muscular organ in the abdomen that receives food from the esophagus. It makes digestive juices and mixes these juices with food to break it down into a thin liquid, which then passes it into the duodenum (the first part of the small intestine).

_Gastric_ means referring to or having to do with the stomach, as in gastric polyp.

, the

duodenum

Close

duodenum

The first part of the small intestine that receives partially digested food from the stomach, absorbs nutrients and passes digested food to the jejunum. The duodenum also receives digestive juices from the pancreas and bile (a yellow-green fluid that helps digest fat) from the liver and gallbladder.

, the

gallbladder

Close

gallbladder

The pear-shaped, muscular organ below the liver that concentrates and stores bile (a yellow-green fluid that helps digest fat).

and part of the

bile duct

Close

bile duct

The tube that carries bile (a yellow-green fluid that helps digest fat) from the liver to the duodenum (the first part of the small intestine).

Two hepatic ducts leave the liver and join to form the common hepatic duct. The cystic duct leaves the gallbladder and joins the common hepatic duct to form the common bile duct.

. The remaining bile duct is attached to the small intestine.

Also called pancreaticoduodenectomy.
