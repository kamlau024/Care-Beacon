# Ann Arbor staging system

## Description

A system that is used to describe the extent of cancer in the body (the stage) for Hodgkin lymphoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL).

Ann Arbor stages are based on the extent to which the cancer has spread in the body, including the lymph nodes and organs affected.

* * *

**Hodgkin lymphoma:**

A type of lymphoma (cancer that starts in immune cells of the lymphatic system ) that is characterized by the presence of Hodgkin and Reed-Sternberg (HRS) cells (large, abnormal cells found only in people with Hodgkin lymphoma).

Also called Hodgkin’s disease.



**non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL):**

A group of related lymphomas (cancer that starts in immune cells of the lymphatic system ) that are not Hodgkin lymphoma .

There are many different types of non-Hodgkin lymphoma. They are grouped by the lymphocyte (a type of white blood cell) they start in. B-cell lymphomas start in B cells (a type of white blood cell that makes antibodies to fight bacteria, viruses and fungi). T-cell lymphomas start in T cells (a type of white blood cell that helps control immune response, fight infection and destroy abnormal cells, including cancer cells).
