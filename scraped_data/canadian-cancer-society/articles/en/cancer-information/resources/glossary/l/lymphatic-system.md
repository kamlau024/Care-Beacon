# lymphatic system

## Description

The group of tissues and organs that produce and store cells that fight infection and diseases.

The lymphatic system includes the adenoids, tonsils, spleen, thymus, lymph nodes, lymph vessels and bone marrow.

Also called the lymph system.

* * *

**adenoids:**

A mass of lymphatic tissue at the back of the throat (behind the nose) that helps the body fight infection by trapping bacteria and viruses.



**tonsils:**

One of 2 small masses of lymphatic tissue on each side of the throat just behind the mouth that help the body fight infection by trapping bacteria and viruses and producing antibodies.



**spleen:**

The organ on the upper-left side of the abdomen near the stomach that makes lymphocytes (a type of white blood cell that fights germs, foreign substances or cancer cells), stores blood cells, filters the blood and destroys old blood cells.



**thymus:**

The small gland in the centre of the chest behind the breastbone (sternum) where lymphocytes (a type of white blood cell that fights germs, foreign substances or cancer cells) mature, multiply and become T cells.

Also called thymus gland.



**lymph nodes:**

A small, bean-shaped mass of lymphatic tissue along lymph vessels (tubes through which lymph fluid travels in the body). Lymph nodes store lymphocytes (a type of white blood cell that fights germs, foreign substances or cancer cells) and filters bacteria and foreign substances (including cancer cells) from lymph fluid.



**lymph vessels:**

A tube through which lymph fluid travels in the body.



**bone marrow:**

The soft, spongy tissue inside most bones.

There are 2 main types of bone marrow. Red bone marrow is where blast cells (immature blood cells) develop into red blood cells , white blood cells and platelets . Yellow bone marrow stores fatty tissue.
